Loading buffer composition
▶

A carrier mix that makes the sample gel-ready:

  • SDS — brings denaturation to the working level
  • Reducing agent (breaks S–S; added fresh — it oxidizes): DTT 100 mM in the sample (in the 4× stock — 200–400 mM) or β-mercaptoethanol 5% v/v in the sample (Laemmli equivalent; in a 2× stock — 10%); interchangeable, mercaptoethanol is weaker — used in molar excess, DTT is odorless and more stable in −20 °C aliquots
  • Glycerol — density: the sample sinks to the well bottom instead of spreading
  • Bromophenol blue — visibility when loading + the dye front
  • Tris-HCl pH 6.8

Compositions and shelf life — on the Buffers page.

Storage
▶

Commercial — freezer (−20 °C) up to 12 months / 4 °C up to 6 months. Homemade: the base without reducing agent keeps for months at RT + DTT on the day of use, or complete aliquots in the freezer.

Arithmetic: volumes
▶

A mini-gel well holds 10–30 µL (1.0 mm/10 wells ≈ 30 µL). Aim for a 10–20 µL final with margin; all lanes — the same final volume.

Example for a 15 µL final: sample 5 µL (10 µg at 2 mg/mL) + 4× buffer 3.75 µL + make-up buffer 6.25 µL.

Heating: how it is actually done
▶

Heat block at 95 °C with Eppendorf sockets, 5 min, hold the caps down → straight onto ice → quick spin 10–30 s.

Classic loss: skipping the spin — up to 20% of the sample stays on the cap. Sensitive proteins: 70 °C 10 min.

Make-up buffer
▶

Equalizing UNEQUAL samples: diluted 1× loading buffer (the cleanest variant), water (when topping up ≤5–10 µL), lysis buffer (if the samples are in it).

Rule: add no extra protein, no SDS beyond the calculation, no excess salts. Needed only when sample concentrations/volumes differ.

Questions from our walkthrough

❓ Do I measure the sample concentration by Bradford/BCA?

Yes — BCA for SDS lysates (Bradford misbehaves with SDS). On an aliquot, BEFORE loading buffer and heating: SDS and DTT both interfere with BCA. Order: the number → arithmetic → buffer → 95 °C.
