Wet transfer (wet tank)
▶

Gel + membrane in a large tank of transfer buffer (~1 L), vertical, 2–3 h at 60–80 V or overnight at 10–30 V, +4 °C.

Large volume = heat removal = long transfers are possible. For proteins >100 kDa and precious samples. The gold standard of completeness.

Semi-dry
▶

Buffer only in the filter paper, horizontal between graphite plates, 15–60 min. Fast, economical; must not be over-run (it dries out — the transfer "burns"). Medium/small proteins.

Dry (Trans-Blot Turbo)
▶

Ready cassettes with pre-soaked buffer, current-controlled instrument, 7–15 min. Standardized, consumables expensive.

Membranes and control
▶

Nitrocellulose — easy blocking; PVDF — higher capacity and strength, mandatory wetting in methanol. Transfer control: Ponceau S (reversible, 5 min), stain-free gels.

In a paper: "overnight at 30 V" = wet; "semi-dry 20 min" = semi-dry.

Questions from our walkthrough

❓ What are the advantages of the methanol transfer buffer?

Methanol (20%, the classic Towbin buffer) does three things. First, it strips SDS off the protein surface: the SDS coat interferes with hydrophobic binding to the membrane, and without methanol small proteins pass straight through nitrocellulose/PVDF. Second, it condenses the gel and narrows the pores — bands do not diffuse and the gel does not swell. Third, it fixes proteins (precipitates them on contact), reducing losses during washes. The price: the same pore narrowing traps large proteins (>100 kDa) — for those, methanol is cut to 10% or omitted (compensated with 0.01–0.05% SDS) and a long wet transfer is used.
