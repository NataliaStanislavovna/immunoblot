BCA
Protein quantification method (bicinchoninic acid, read at 562 nm): tolerant of detergents, incompatible with reducing agents.
Bradford
Protein concentration method via Coomassie binding (595 nm): fast, but incompatible with SDS.
cathepsins
Lysosomal proteases: released during lysis — the reason protease inhibitors are mandatory.
CMC
Critical micelle concentration of a detergent: the minimum concentration at which micelles form and membrane dissolution proceeds properly.
conformational epitope
An epitope formed by the tertiary structure of a protein: destroyed by SDS denaturation — hence native recombinant antigens in diagnostics.
direct/indirect/sandwich ELISA
ELISA formats: antigen on the plate + labeled antibodies (direct); labeled secondaries (indirect, the most common); antigen between two antibodies (sandwich, the most specific).
dot blot
Sample as a dot on the membrane without separation: fast, but the protein is unknown — a yes/no answer.
DTT
Dithiothreitol: a reducing agent that breaks S–S disulfide bridges. Oxidizes in air — added fresh.
ECL
Chemiluminescent detection: HRP + luminol → light. Sensitivity down to femtograms.
ELISA
Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay: an enzyme label colors the well reaction; the result is optical density (a number). A screening method: sensitive, but with cross-reactivity.
Gel-loading tips
Tips with a long thin geometry for loading gel wells; regular Eppendorf tips do not fit.
housekeeping proteins
Constitutive normalizer proteins (actin, GAPDH, tubulin): the old standard; the weak spot — they change in the experiment themselves.
HRP
Horseradish peroxidase: the enzyme label for ECL and chromogenic detection; inhibited by sodium azide.
IFA
Immunofluorescence assay: a fluorophore label (FITC), reaction on cells/tissue, read by microscope — a staining pattern. Qualitative/semi-quantitative.
line blot
A diagnostic format: antigens as lines in fixed positions on a membrane; serum gives an antibody profile; the confirmatory test after ELISA/IFA.
loading buffer
Sample loading buffer: SDS + reducing agent + glycerol (density) + bromophenol blue (indicator). 4× or 6×.
make-up buffer
An addition for equalizing the final lane volume (diluted loading buffer, water, lysis buffer) when sample concentrations differ.
metalloproteases
Proteases with a metal ion (Zn²⁺) in the active site: inhibited by EDTA (chelates the metal).
NIR
Near-infrared range (~700–800 nm): fluorescence detection with a wide linear range (Li-Cor Odyssey), two channels simultaneously.
nitrocellulose
The classic membrane: gentle protein binding, easy blocking.
Ponceau S
A reversible transfer-control dye: stains all membrane proteins in 5 minutes, washes off with water.
PVDF
Polyvinylidene fluoride: a membrane with high capacity and strength; requires wetting in methanol.
running buffer
Electrode buffer (Tris-glycine-SDS): carries the current and sets the migration conditions.
SDS
Sodium dodecyl sulfate: an anionic detergent. Dissolves membranes, unfolds proteins, binds at 1.4 g/g, imparts a uniform negative charge — the basis of size-based separation.
SDS-PAGE
Electrophoresis of proteins in a polyacrylamide gel after SDS treatment — separation by molecular weight.
smiling
"Smiling" lanes: the edges run faster than the center due to overheating or poorly mixed buffer.
stacking gel
The upper 4% gel part with large pores: packs the sample into a narrow starting zone for a sharp entry into the resolving gel.
TBS-T
Wash buffer: Tris-buffered saline + NaCl + Tween-20 (a detergent that washes off unbound material).
transfer buffer
Transfer buffer (Tris-glycine + 20% methanol): methanol helps protein binding to the membrane.
Western blot
= immunoblotting: protein separation, transfer to a membrane, detection with antibodies. Named by analogy with Southern blot.
β-mercaptoethanol
The second classic reducer of S–S bridges, pungent smell.
